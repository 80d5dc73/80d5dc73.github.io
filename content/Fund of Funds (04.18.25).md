Idea: 
1. Target low-capital investors with the promise of higher interest returns (something like Citadel, where it's >18%). 
2. Essentially, a group buy similar to a Wealthfront structure.
	Wealthfront structure: average Joe can invest $x$ amount of money and achieve 4% HYSA returns instantly. Same capital would fetch 2% in a regular savings account. However, able to fetch higher rates by grouping into a single batch and going out to invest. 

**We are basically a Citadel wrapper.** We wouldn't be the first stupid wrapper to make it.

**How would we do it?**
Alice comes to us with $2500. We promise that we'll get her some portion of a hedge fund (say Citadel $x$). $x$ achieves a return of $a$. We take some small cut of $a$ and return the rest of the money to Alice. 

**What's the problem?** 
Managing money is non-trivial. We'd have to delicately manage everyone's money. 

Possible issues:
1. Having people able to withdraw with some liquidity. This would require us to have some liquidity ourselves, possibly diluting the returns. That's fine. 
2. Answering to a lot of people. The main issue that Citadel has no interest in something like this is that they don't want to deal with Alice calling on a Wednesday to ask where her money is. Instead, we'd have to build a platform which answered these questions. 
3. Legal regulations: we'd have to register as a fund (likely). This would be extremely expensive... however, with enough promise of a product and possible users, we'd have no worry. My one issue that is with enough marketing, these funds would catch on and would trample us in the time it takes for legality processions. Eh, who cares, I'd like to trample the large. 
4. Getting Citadel (or any fund for that matter) to have interest in us. They like money. If we have money, we have a spot :)

We are the intercessory. We aren't doing trading. We are simply managing money and acting as **as a vehicle** to redistribute funds. It can happens for banks and HYSAs, it can happen for hedge funds, PE, etc.
